Design Decisions

  • All three share: bottom sheet (not fullscreen modal), drag handle, dim overlay at 30%, warm #fafaf8 paper bg, Noto Serif JP headlines, "Not now" + "Restore Purchase" as quiet text links, no dark patterns
  • No fullscreen takeover: the Combinations screen is visible behind the dim — the user sees where they are, doesn't feel kidnapped
  • Price always visible: €0.99 shown clearly, never hidden behind the CTA
  • One-time purchase emphasis: every proposal makes it clear this is NOT a subscription — reduces purchase anxiety
  • "Not now" is always available: dismissable by swipe-down, tap "Not now", or tap dimmed area — three escape routes

Proposal Comparison

  • A (The Wada Page): Most minimal. Feels like a page from Wada's book. The mini-palette is the only visual element. Kanji お気に入り (favorites) as accent. Risk: might feel too sparse
  • B (The Warm Invitation): Warmest. Gold accent gradient, benefit list, "support independent development" emotional appeal. Most informative. Risk: benefit list could feel generic
  • C (The Collector): Most contextual. Shows the user's actual saved palettes, the 6th one faded as "what you're about to lose." Strongest emotional trigger. Risk: slightly more complex

Interaction Details

  • Trigger: User taps ♡ on their 6th combination (after 5 free saves)
  • Animation: Sheet slides up with spring damping (300ms), dim fades in (200ms)
  • Dismiss: Swipe down / tap "Not now" / tap dim area → spring dismiss
  • After dismiss: Paywall does NOT appear again in the same session
  • Haptic: .rigid on purchase confirmed, .light on dismiss
  • CTA tap: Triggers StoreKit 2 purchase sheet (Face ID / Apple Pay)
